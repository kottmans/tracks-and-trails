What to do next, in order

One ordering, and the reason for it. The numbers are a sequence rather than a ranking: each item unblocks or de-risks the one under it.

  1. T-105 Write docs/UX_SPEC.md Unblocks 5

    It gates T-107, T-109, T-110, T-111 and T-112, and it is the only thing on the board that does. UX-005 has been the authority in its place since 2026-08-03 and says so in its own consequences. Until this is written the phase can only do work it found by accident.

  2. T-107 The format table Foundation

    Named High because REQ-008 and T-109 both act on this table. It also owns proving the phase's first exit criterion — the table matching yt-dlp -F for a fixture set — so it carries a gate as well as a feature.

  3. T-113 Resume across a restart Start early

    Placed third despite being startable today, and deliberately not last: it reopens two accepted decisions and carries the phase's highest uncertainty. Discovering what it costs late is how a phase overruns — and it needs no spec, so nothing is lost by learning early.

  4. T-108 Video plus audio, merged After T-107

    Reads the table T-107 builds, and settles the ffmpeg-presence rule that T-109 then shares. Two exit criteria run through here: the merge itself, on both platforms.

  5. T-110 Playlists, properly Structural

    Changes what a job is, so it reaches core/ rather than the UI. Worth taking while the queue and History surfaces are freshly reviewed — T-137, T-140 and T-145 have each already moved part of the playlist story.

  6. T-109 Post-processing, seven options Largest

    The biggest item in the phase, and the one with a documented history of failing quietly: T-077 found four of five Phase 1 options never produced a file. Late in the order because it wants T-108's ffmpeg rule settled first.

T-111, T-112 and T-114 are not in this list because nothing about their order is contentious: T-111 follows T-109, T-112 follows the spec, and T-114 can be taken by anyone at any point.

Two accepted decisions were reopened to get here

Both were proposed by the implementer and ratified separately

  • UX-005 — a finished playlist is one History row. Three sub-decisions: the chip counts, there is no segmented bar, and a partly-failed playlist says 14 items rather than 14 of 16, because a stored original count goes stale the first time a user removes a member.
  • DAT-005 §1 — reopened to admit clearing the whole list. It had refused Clear all and named the condition that would lift the refusal; T-125 met it. The verb is Clear history, because all has no object and the one a user supplies is their files.

Both were first recorded as maintainer rulings that had not happened, caught in review as T145-R1 and T144-R1. The content survived ratification unchanged; the attribution was the defect. Phase 3 has more of this ahead — T-113 reopens UX-001 by design.
