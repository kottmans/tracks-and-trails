## `T132-R2` — the repolish and the claim behind it

**The finding.** `d929d98` added `style().unpolish()` / `style().polish()` to the toolbar's primary
action, with a comment asserting that a dynamic property set after the first polish is ignored.
That mechanism was `T132-R1`'s, and the re-review **withdrew `T132-R1` as reviewer error**: it had
measured a window built by `_window_over()`, which supplies no job sink and no output directory, so
`T-016` disabled the action and `UX-005` row 6 correctly paints a disabled primary `sunken`. The
repolish was therefore unnecessary behaviour resting on a false explanation.

**What changed.**

- The `unpolish`/`polish` pair is deleted, and the comment stating the disproved mechanism as fact
  is replaced by one recording why there is no repolish and why there should not be a new one.
- The regression `test_the_windows_real_add_button_is_filled_after_the_toolbar_builds_it` is
  **kept**, with its docstring, inline comment and failure message rewritten. All three asserted
  the disproved mechanism; a test whose stated purpose is false is the `T132-R1` shape one level
  down.

**What the test is for now, stated plainly.** It is the only assertion that the **enabled** primary
action is brand-filled in a window composed the way the application composes one — as opposed to
the isolated style test, which sets `primaryAction` on a bare button of its own. That composition
is what it earns its place for, and the measurement `T132-R1` misread is recorded in the comment:
property `True`, **disabled `#EAEFE9`, enabled `#1E5E47`**.
