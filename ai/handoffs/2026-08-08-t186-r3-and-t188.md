## 2 · `T-188` — a recorded pair, and an honest label

**The maintainer ruled on the surveyed candidate: take it and label it honestly.**

`tests/fixtures/infodicts/dash_akamai_big_buck_bunny.json` — ten video-only formats carrying
`acodec: 'none'` beside one audio-only carrying `vcodec: 'none'`. **Captured through
`tests/fixtures/capture.py`**, so the `extractor` declaration is cross-checked against what yt-dlp
reported and cannot drift (`T107-R8`'s gate).

**What it closes.** Until now `kind_of`'s routing met only shapes this project authored:
`derived_format_columns` is synthetic and says so, and `T-108`'s end-to-end case drives a local HLS
presentation this project generates. A rule that only ever meets its own inputs has no evidence
behind it.

**What it does not close, and where that is written.** The extractor is `generic` — yt-dlp parses
the `.mpd` directly rather than running site-specific code — and the licence is known from the
content's identity rather than stated by the source. **Both are in the fixture's own `why` and
`content_licence` blocks**, not only in `TASKS.md`, so a reader meets the limitation without
knowing to look for it.

**Why `generic` is the right seam anyway**, which is the argument you should press on if any:
the literal `'none'` *originates* in DASH parsing. An `AdaptationSet` declares `mimeType`
`video/mp4` or `audio/mp4`; yt-dlp fills the codec the representation lacks with `'none'` rather
than leaving it unknown; `_as_stream_presence` maps that to `False`. So this pins the exact
mechanism the routing depends on. A site extractor would be *additional* evidence, not different.

**Both fixtures are kept** — the synthetic one names its own shapes and stays legible; the recorded
one shows something published has them.

**Mutation-checked:** making `_as_stream_presence` read `'none'` as a codec rather than an absence
fails both new cases, plus twelve existing ones.
