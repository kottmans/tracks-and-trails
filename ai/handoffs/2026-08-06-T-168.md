## Why it happened, since neither contributing task is wrong

`T-163` made the **verbs** the half that yields to the progress bar — correct, because a dropped
verb is still in `⋯` and a squeezed bar has no overflow. `T-164` **merges** a narrow bar from
sixteen blocks to eight — also correct, because sixteen blocks at 15 px is one bar.

Composed, the bar's demand became a step function of the row's width, and the step is bigger than a
button: `segment_span(16)` is 271 px and `segment_span(8)` is 135 px, so crossing the merge
threshold handed the verbs **136 px** back for being made *smaller*.

**`T-167`'s monotonicity claim is narrowly true and did not cover this.** `_bar_line`'s docstring
says *"monotonic in the window's width … everything subtracted here is fixed for a given row"* —
true of `_bar_line`, which subtracts only the `⋯`. What was not monotonic is `_bar_reserve`, which
called `segment_blocks`, and therefore what was left for the verbs. **The property the user
experiences was never the one asserted**, which is the part worth carrying forward.

## The fix

One line: `_bar_reserve` asks for `segment_span(entries)` — the bar's **widest** rendering — instead
of `segment_span(segment_blocks(entries, room))`, the rendering this width happened to choose.

The verbs' share becomes `max(0, area.width() - segment_span(entries) - VERB_GAP)`, which is
**continuous** in the row's width, not merely monotone: at the crossing both expressions equal
`⋯ + 1`, so there is no step at all.

**The bar is not narrowed by this**, and that is worth checking rather than taking from me. The bar
rect is `area.left() … verbs_left` — whatever the verbs leave — and `_paint_segments` still chooses
its block count from `_bar_line`. So below the threshold the bar takes the whole line and draws
eight *wider* blocks, which is more of `T-164` rather than less. Above it, nothing changes.

**Direction 3 from the task entry was rejected on sight and the entry records why**: making the drop
sticky per row would make what a row shows depend on the widths it has previously been drawn at,
which is the paint-time-state defect `T-135` already had to correct.

## Evidence

`test_a_verb_dropped_at_one_width_never_returns_at_a_narrower_one`.

**Swept, not sampled** — `SWEEP_WIDTHS` is every pixel from 300 to 1200, walked downward, asserting
that the dropped set only ever grows. The step is one threshold wide, so a test checking three
chosen widths walks straight over it; that is how this survived `T-167`. The assertion is on the
*shape* of the sequence rather than on any width's value, so a font or label change cannot make it
wrong in a way that hides the defect.

It also asserts that something was dropped at the narrowest width, so a change that made every verb
fit everywhere could not pass it vacuously.
