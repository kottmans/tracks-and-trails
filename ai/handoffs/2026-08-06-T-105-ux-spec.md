## Read this first: twelve clauses are Proposed, and that is the design

The document marks every clause **[T]** transcribed, **[D]** derived, or **[P]** proposed, and
collects all twelve `[P]`s in §10 with the rule that **no task may build one until it is ratified**.

That structure exists because of your own findings. `T145-R1` and `T144-R1` were an implementer
choosing product decisions and recording them as maintainer rulings. This file is eight surfaces
written before anything is built — the same failure mode with eight times the surface — so the
separation is built in rather than applied afterwards.

**The question I most want tested is whether I have marked them honestly.** A `[D]` that is really a
`[P]` is the defect this whole scheme exists to prevent, and I am the wrong person to audit my own
marking. Specifically worth challenging:

- **§6's central `[D]`** — that `REQ-010`'s options are preset-owned, so the post-processing surface
  *is* the preset editor's rather than a panel of its own. I claim it derives from
  `PRESET_OWNED_FIELDS` and `to_request()`'s refusal to override a preset-owned field
  (`T015-R1`). If that is a product choice rather than a derivation, `T-109` and `T-111` are two
  screens and §6 is wrong.
- **§7's `[D]`** that the playlist picker is the staging row opened, not a dialog — derived from
  `UX-005` §3's one-anatomy rule and the fact that the queue and History already draw a playlist
  that way. It could be argued as `[P]`.
- **§5's `[D]`** that the ffmpeg check reads the resulting selector rather than the chosen format.
  I believe this is `T-061` verbatim, not a new choice.

**A correction I made while writing, which is the kind of thing to look for more of:** §6 first said
*"six of the seven post-processing options are already fields on `Preset`"*. That is false — only
two have dedicated fields (audio codec/quality, subtitles); the other five have none and would ride
