## `T108-R1` — the criterion is met as a file, and building it corrected the code

You were right that a `DownloadRequest` is not a merged file, and that the span between them is
where `T-061` lived.

`test_a_chosen_video_and_audio_pair_produce_one_merged_file` drives **the user's whole route** —
paste, resolve, open the control, choose *Choose specific formats…*, tick the merge mode, pick each
half — against a **local HLS presentation whose video and audio are separate renditions**
(`build_split_hls`). `hls_media_url`'s existing presentation carries both streams in one variant and
can never need merging, which is why a new one was needed rather than a new selector over the old
one. Then `ffprobe` reads the output: **one file, `['audio', 'video']`**.

**It runs on both platforms** — nothing in it is POSIX-only, and CI runs the whole suite on Windows.
Linux is verified here; the Windows half is CI's to report, and I have not claimed it.

**Two mutations, because a green end-to-end test proves nothing until it has failed.** Dropping the

### Building it found a defect in the routing rule

**An `EXT-X-MEDIA:TYPE=AUDIO` group arrives as `vcodec: 'none'` with no `acodec` at all**, because
HLS puts the codec list on the *variant* rather than on the group. `kind_of` required both answers
to be known, so **the commonest real audio half was unpairable** — the mode would not even have been
offered. The rule now keys on the explicit `'none'` and tolerates silence on the other side, while
still refusing silence *alone*: media.ccc.de's `vcodec: 'h264'` with no `acodec` stays `UNKNOWN`,
and a format denied *both* streams — a storyboard — is neither half.

`is_audio_only`/`is_video_only` carry the rule and `kind_of` asks them, so the projection and the
routing cannot come to disagree; a test asserts that correspondence across every format of every
fixture. Both new shapes are in `derived_format_columns`, declared synthetic.

**This is the finding behind the finding, and it is worth your attention**: the rule looked right,
was reviewed, and was wrong about real data. Only an end-to-end test through a real extractor could
have said so.

## `T108-R2` — remounted by identity, and a second Qt collision underneath it

Your probe reproduced exactly. `remount_format_table` puts the panel back on its row **by identity**
after `endResetModel`, or closes it (keeping the choice) when the row is gone. Positional would be
wrong for precisely the A/B → B/A case you used: row 0 stays valid and means a different URL.

Five tests: reordering, adding a line, removing the *other* line, removing the panel's **own** line,
and reopening after a reset. Each also asserts your second symptom directly — asking to open the
already-open row leaves it *mounted*, not blank. Removing the remount fails four of the five.

**And a collision I had not seen.** `setIndexWidget` and item editors share one map in
`QAbstractItemView`, so mounting the panel from inside `setData` — which Qt calls during
`commitData`, with the combo box still open — **destroyed the editor under the delegate**. The next
structural reset then called `commitData` on it: *libshiboken: Internal C++ object already deleted*.
Your finding's own tests are what surfaced it, since nothing before them opened a panel and then
reset. The mount is deferred by one event-loop turn, which is the only ordering in which both the
editor and the widget can exist.
