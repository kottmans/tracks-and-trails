## `T-115` — the queue now runs itself

Nothing drained it. `_fill_free_slots` drains `_waiting`, which is in-memory and only the internal
parking path ever filled; `start()` raised at saturation; the add dialog started only the job it had
probed. Five URLs and a limit of three left two `QUEUED` for ever; five URLs with no probe started
nothing at all.

**`DownloadManager.admit()`** is the public counterpart to `start()`. `start()` raises when full,
and is right to — its caller asked for a session *now*. `admit()` expresses durable intent instead,
so adding five URLs to a pool of three does not make the caller decide which two to drop. The
parking primitive already existed; it had no public door.

Two callers, per your own recommendation — manager-owned, with one startup admission:

- `AddUrlDialog` admits **every** job it persisted, not only the probed one.
- `compose()` admits durable `QUEUED` rows **after** `recover_interrupted()`.

**That ordering is the whole safety argument.** Recovery moves in-flight rows to `FAILED` first, so
nothing that was *running* when the application died is admitted — it is offered for retry
(`T-082`). Starting those was `T081-R4`, and this is the rule that would have resurrected it.

Order stays `queue_position`'s, through `_next_waiting`. A paused queue admits and starts nothing.

### The one thing I would check hardest

**`admit()` reports a missing row through `start_rejected` rather than raising.** `start()` raises
for the same case and should: its caller named a job and a vanished row means their model is wrong.
`admit()` runs in Qt slots — the dialog's save callback and composition's startup loop — where an
exception is printed and swallowed, and where a row may not have landed yet. **Your contended-database
test is what caught this**; I had inherited `start()`'s contract without thinking about the caller.

Judge whether reporting is right, or whether the dialog should instead not admit until the write is
visible. I chose reporting because the alternative makes the dialog responsible for the store's
timing.

## `T-088` — your `T088-R4` and `T087-R6`, corrected

- **`all([])` is `True`.** My read-only observer returned `{}` on a persistent read failure, so one
  missed sample would have reported every job terminal, turned the strict xfail into an `XPASS`, and
  **announced a repair that had not happened**. `settled()` and `snapshot()` require every id
  present before answering; `settled([])` is explicitly `False`; `snapshot()` raises. All three
  tested directly.
- **`reap_application()` reaps the captured tree at all four sites**, and `application_pid` is
  **required** — an optional one with a `Popen` fallback reinstates launcher-only teardown silently.
- **`_kill_tree()` asserts `wait_procs(...).alive` is empty**, and suppresses only `NoSuchProcess`
  while walking and `ProcessLookupError` on the trailing kill. `process.wait()` raises on timeout.
- **`T087-R6`:** the holder reports its own pid, the test kills *that* process and waits, and the
  `reap` fixture takes the tree. **I had called this flaky twice with the evidence in front of me.**

Writing the reap test found that `kill_the_application` reaps the process *group* on POSIX, so a
child spawned without `isolate_the_application()` takes pytest down with it. The first version
SIGKILLed its own runner — exit 137, no report.

## One thing the mutation battery could not show

**Pause is enforced at three sites** — `_start_when_free`, `start()` and `_fill_free_slots` — and
each alone suffices, so removing any *one* is unobservable. That is redundancy in the code, not a
gap in the tests, and inventing a test that could tell them apart would be testing an implementation
detail. The battery mutates the flag all three read, which puts the **property** under test. Whether
three checks earn their keep is `T-080`'s question; this task neither added nor removed them.
